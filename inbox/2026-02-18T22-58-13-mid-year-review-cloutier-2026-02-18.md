# Mid-Year Review with Dr. Robert Cloutier — 2026-02-18

## Key Themes

### 1. Ambient Scribe as Liberation, Not Threat

Central thesis: ambient scribes don't erode clinical thinking — they *create space for it*. Current documentation is a medico-legal exercise, not a clinical reasoning exercise. The cognitive load isn't "thinking about the patient" — it's "remembering to document what I thought about the patient." Offloading the secretarial burden doesn't remove the thinking; it removes the distraction *from* thinking.

Reframes the dominant fear narrative. Counterargument to "AI scribes will make us lazy thinkers": we're not currently doing rigorous thinking in our notes anyway — we're doing compliance documentation.

### 2. Historical Arc of ED Documentation (Rob's 40-Year Perspective)

- Handwritten T-charts: **efficient but not effective** (brief, lacked depth)
- Current EMR era: **effective for legal purposes but not efficient** (verbose, time-consuming, not clinically oriented)
- Ambient scribes: potential third path — captures clinical nuance without the cognitive tax

### 3. Medical Education Implications

If faculty/residents aren't spending cognitive surplus on documentation, what happens to teaching? Hypothesis: ambient scribes could revitalize bedside teaching and clinical reasoning discussions in academic EDs. Under-explored angle in the literature.

### 4. Focus Groups & Thematic Analysis

Rob endorsed qualitative methods — focus groups with thematic analysis — to capture spectrum of adoption behaviors. Segment by adoption level: non-users, low adopters, high adopters, super users. Layer onto existing AI scribe survey (with Jordan Wackett) for mixed-methods depth.
