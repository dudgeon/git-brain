## Core Thesis
Ambient AI scribes don't erode clinical reasoning — they offload the secretarial distraction FROM critical thinking. Current documentation is medico-legal compliance, not clinical reasoning. The question isn't "what do we lose?" but "what do we gain?"

## Key Arguments
1. **The Lawyer Analogy:** No one expects lawyers to transcribe their own trials — why do we expect physicians to document their own encounters?
2. **Historical Arc:** T-charts (efficient/not effective) → EMR (effective for legal/not efficient) → Ambient scribe (potential third path)
3. **Cognitive Science:** Real-time documentation is a compensatory strategy for working memory limits, not a reasoning tool
4. **Privacy Paradox:** Medicine became self-documenting due to privacy culture, but AI may resolve this better than human scribes
5. **Education Opportunity:** Freed cognitive space → bedside teaching, clinical reasoning discussions, deliberate practice

## Key Evidence Points
- ED physicians: 44% of time on data entry, 4,000 clicks/shift (Hill et al., 2013)
- Only 12-13% of intern time on direct patient contact
- Kaiser: 15,700+ hours saved across 2.5M encounters
- Stanford: significant reductions in task load and burnout
- 1995/1997 E/M guidelines explicitly rewarded documentation volume over quality
